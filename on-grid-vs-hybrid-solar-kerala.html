On-grid vs hybrid solar: which suits your home?

Most Kerala homes choose on-grid solar because it gives the biggest savings for the money. A hybrid system with a battery makes sense when power cuts are a real problem. Here is how to decide.

Why on-grid shuts off in a power cut

A safety rule, not a fault.

When KSEB supply fails, an on-grid inverter stops sending power so that line workers repairing the grid are not hurt by electricity coming back from your roof. That is why an on-grid system gives no backup, even on a sunny day.

If you already have an inverter and battery for backup, it keeps working as before with on-grid solar. Many Kerala homes keep their existing UPS for lights and fans and add on-grid solar to cut the bill.

Our advice

Start from your power cuts.

If power cuts in your area are short and occasional, choose on-grid. It costs the least, gets the full subsidy, and the savings pay back fastest.

If you have long or frequent cuts, run a home office, or have medical equipment, choose hybrid and size the battery for only the loads that matter, such as lights, fans, the fridge and Wi-Fi.

Questions we hear

Frequently asked questions.

Does on-grid solar work during a power cut?
No. An on-grid inverter switches off when KSEB supply fails, for the safety of line workers. For backup you need a hybrid system with a battery, or your existing UPS.
Do hybrid solar systems get the PM Surya Ghar subsidy?
Grid-connected hybrid systems are eligible on their solar panel capacity, up to ₹78,000. The battery cost is not subsidised. Off-grid systems are not covered.
Is a hybrid system worth the extra cost in Kerala?
Only if power cuts are a real problem where you live. For most homes with steady supply, on-grid solar saves more money for the same budget.
Can I add a battery later?
Yes, if you choose a hybrid-ready inverter at the start. Tell us on the site visit if you might want a battery in future.
