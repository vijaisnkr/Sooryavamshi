Who it suits

Right for homes using 450 units a month or more.

A 5 kW system suits homes with two or three air conditioners, a large family, daily induction cooking, or an electric car charged at home. If your KSEB bill shows 450 to 550 units a month (900 to 1,100 units for a two-month bill), 5 kW covers most of it.

The subsidy is the same ₹78,000 as for 3 kW, but higher-use homes pay more per unit under KSEB's slab rates, so each unit solar replaces is worth more to them.

Whether 5 kW runs on your single-phase connection or needs three phase depends on your connected load and KSEB's rules. Load enhancement or three-phase conversion, if needed, is quoted separately.

Questions we hear

Frequently asked questions.

What is the price of a 5 kW solar system in Kerala?
Our typical estimate is ₹2,80,000 to ₹3,10,000 before the ₹78,000 PM Surya Ghar subsidy, worked out from our 3 kW and 8 kW proposals. KSEB fees are extra. We give a fixed price after the free site visit.
How many units does a 5 kW solar system produce?
About 20 to 25 units on a clear day, and about 17 to 18 units a day averaged across the year including the monsoon, which is around 520 units a month.
Is the subsidy higher for a 5 kW system?
No. The PM Surya Ghar subsidy for a home stops at ₹78,000, which you already reach at 3 kW. A 5 kW system with DCR panels gets the same ₹78,000.
How much roof space does 5 kW need?
About 500 sq.ft of shade-free roof for 9 panels.
Should I choose 3 kW or 5 kW?
Choose by your units. Around 300 to 400 units a month suits 3 kW; 450 units or more suits 5 kW. Our solar calculator works it out from your KSEB bill.
